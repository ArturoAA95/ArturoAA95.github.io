Research

My research is in probability theory. I study how spatial inhomogeneity shapes the long-time behaviour of random systems: branching Brownian motion and branching diffusions in periodic environments, diffusions conditioned to stay positive, and scaling limits of interacting particle systems of Smoluchowski type.

A recurring question is how fast, and in what shape, a branching population spreads when the medium varies in space. With L. Addario-Berry and J. Lin, I proved a shape theorem for branching Brownian motion in a periodic environment, and I have since proved a shape theorem for periodic branching diffusions.

I pair proofs with computation. I test theoretical predictions against large Monte Carlo simulations and numerical PDE solvers, written in Python and released as open-source code.

  • 2023–25

    Monte Carlo validation of front speeds for branching populations

    Simulations of a branching particle system with millions of particles, comparing Monte Carlo estimates of the front and cloud velocity with numerical PDE solutions. Filtering non-informative particles kept the cost under control at long time horizons.
