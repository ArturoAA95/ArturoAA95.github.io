Arturo Arellano Arias

Ph.D. candidate in Mathematics
Department of Mathematics and Statistics, McGill University
Advisors: Jessica Lin and Louigi Addario-Berry

I am a Ph.D. candidate at McGill University working in probability. My research interests include branching processes and scaling limits of interacting particle systems. Throughout my Ph.D., I have been fortunate to have the support of the Fonds de recherche du Québec – Nature et technologies (FRQNT) through a doctoral research scholarship.
Before coming to McGill, I completed a Master’s degree in Applied Mathematics at CIMAT, Mexico, and a Bachelor’s degree in Mathematics at the Universidad de Guanajuato, in a program designed and taught jointly with CIMAT. I will graduate in 2027 and am on the job market for postdoctoral positions.

Computation and code

  • 2023–25

    Monte Carlo simulations of binary branching Brownian motion in general heterogeneous environments

    Python code for simulating binary branching Brownian motion in general heterogeneous environments; my largest runs have followed populations of 50 million particles. An optional filter discards non-informative particles, which allows for simulation of longer time horizons.

    Repository

  • 2020–21

    An iterative scheme for a nonlinear transmission problem

    Master's project at CIMAT. An iterative numerical method for a nonlinear PDE, tested on 18 two-dimensional cases on 250×250 grids and checked against Monte Carlo simulation.

    Repository
